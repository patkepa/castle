# Design principles

## Invite, do not rank

The Atlas should help someone participate, not declare which place is “best.”
Amara's phrase—*a map is an invitation, not an inventory*—is the clearest test.

## Show uncertainty

An observation, a recurring pattern, and a hypothesis are different kinds of
knowledge. The [[projects/neighborhood_atlas/data_dictionary|data dictionary]]
keeps those distinctions visible.

## Make room for serendipity

Useful navigation can be precise without eliminating surprise. Related notes,
backlinks, and the graph should reward wandering.

## Protect the quiet corner

Every public activity needs a lower-stimulation way to contribute. This is why
[[tasks/prepare_open_studio|the open-studio task]] includes a quiet feedback station.

## Prefer reversible decisions

Start with simple Markdown, visible links, and portable files. This follows the
logic of [[wiki/local_first_knowledge|Local-first knowledge]].

These principles were reviewed in [[notes/meeting_notes_august|August studio notes]]
and tested during the [[journal/2026-08-27_field_day|field day]].
